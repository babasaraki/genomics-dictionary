Contribute

The dictionary needs subject specialists more than it needs software. There are three ways to help: suggest a missing term, draft an entry, or review entries in your field.

Who can contribute

Anyone with demonstrable expertise in a relevant field — genetics, genomics, bioinformatics, laboratory medicine, public health, pharmacogenomics, ethics or governance — may draft entries. Scientific review is carried out by named reviewers appointed by the editorial team. A contributor cannot approve or publish their own entry; that separation is part of the editorial policy.

What a good entry looks like

  1. A plain-language definition of roughly 40 to 80 words that a first-year student can follow.
  2. A technical explanation of roughly 100 to 250 words, including at least one limitation of the concept.
  3. A practical example from a laboratory, clinic, classroom or surveillance setting — never identifiable patient material.
  4. A common misunderstanding, stated and then corrected.
  5. Three to six related concepts already in the dictionary.
  6. Term-specific references with a URL or DOI, not a glossary homepage.

Write original prose. Adapted text must be documented. Avoid implying that any genome is normal or representative, keep association separate from causation, and distinguish germline from somatic findings.

How drafting and review work today

The corpus is held as structured JSON in the project repository. An entry is drafted, validated against the schema, reviewed by someone other than its author, and only then marked as approved and published. The current 200 entries completed that step in October 2026 and carry the reviewer, the reviewed revision and a next-review date; they fall due for re-review in October 2027. New and revised entries follow the same route, and a revision made after approval does not reach the public site until it has been reviewed again.
