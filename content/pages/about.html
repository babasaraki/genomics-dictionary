About The Genomics Dictionary

An open-access dictionary that explains genomics terminology twice: once in plain language, and once for a reader who needs the technical detail.

Why it exists

Genomics borrows vocabulary from genetics, molecular biology, computing, statistics, clinical medicine and public health. A reader can recognise a term without knowing its limits or how it relates to neighbouring concepts. Very short definitions are too thin for professional use; technical explanations alone are hard to enter as a beginner. Every entry here therefore carries a concise plain-language definition, an expanded technical explanation, a practical example, a common misunderstanding, related concepts and references.

Who it is for

Students and early-career scientists meeting terminology for the first time; researchers checking precise meaning and limitations; laboratory and bioinformatics staff interpreting methods and outputs; health and public health professionals reading applied genomic information; and lecturers who need stable links and printable entries for teaching. The dictionary connects human genomics, pathogen genomics and bioinformatics, and uses African examples where they make an explanation more useful.

What it is not

It does not interpret anyone's genetic results, support diagnostic decisions, accept genomic data uploads or provide clinical advice. Definitions explain concepts; decisions about testing or treatment belong with qualified professionals who know the full context.

Current status

Reviewed corpus. All 200 entries were drafted with AI assistance and have since been read and approved by the project's scientific lead, Dr Umar Ahmad, under the editorial policy. Each entry page records the reviewer, the revision that was reviewed, the date of review and the date the next review falls due. Entries are scheduled for re-review every 12 months, and any entry revised after approval returns for renewed review before it is republished.
