    A few entries

    • ACMG/AMP variant classification

      ACMG/AMP variant classification is a shared framework that clinical laboratories use to decide how confident they are that a DNA variant causes disease. It sorts evidence into weighted criteria and combines them into five tiers: pathogenic, likely pathogenic, uncertain significance, likely benign and benign. The aim is that different laboratories reading the same evidence reach the same conclusion.

      Clinical and cancer genomics Published

    • Admixture

      Admixture happens when people from populations that had been separated for a long time have children together, so their descendants carry chromosome segments of different ancestral origin. Over successive generations recombination cuts these segments into shorter pieces. Most human populations today show some degree of admixture, and it is a feature of a genome's history rather than a defect.

      Human and population genomics Ethics and data governance Published

    • Adverse drug reaction (ADR)

      An adverse drug reaction, abbreviated ADR, is harm caused by a medicine given at a dose used for treatment or prevention. It differs from a medication error, where the wrong drug or dose was given. Some reactions are predictable extensions of the drug's action; others are rare, severe and immune-mediated, and a few are strongly associated with inherited variation.

      Pharmacogenomics Published

    • Allele

      An allele is one of the alternative versions of a sequence found at a particular place in the genome. Because people inherit one copy of each chromosome from each parent, an individual carries two alleles at most positions, which may be identical or different. Alleles can differ by a single letter or by a larger stretch of sequence.

      Genetics foundations Published

    • Amplicon sequencing

      Amplicon sequencing is a method in which chosen regions of a genome are copied many times by the polymerase chain reaction and only those copies are sequenced. Because the target is enriched before sequencing, very small amounts of pathogen material can be read even when a sample is dominated by human or environmental DNA. Tiled sets of overlapping primers can cover a whole small genome.

      Pathogen genomics Published

    • Aneuploidy

      Aneuploidy means having a number of chromosomes that differs from the usual complement for that organism, such as one extra copy or one missing copy. In humans it usually arises when chromosomes fail to separate correctly during the formation of eggs or sperm. Aneuploidy is also very common in cancer cells, where whole chromosomes are frequently gained or lost.

      Genetics foundations Published
