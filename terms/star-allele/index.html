Star allele nomenclature

Pharmacogenomics Published

Definition

Star allele nomenclature is the naming system used for versions of genes that affect how medicines are processed. Each named version, written with an asterisk and a number such as star two, stands for a defined combination of variants inherited together on one chromosome. It names a haplotype rather than a single DNA change.

Technical explanation

Star alleles are haplotypes defined by curated combinations of variants, including single-nucleotide variants, small insertions and deletions, gene deletions, duplications and hybrid genes formed with neighbouring pseudogenes. Definitions are maintained by nomenclature consortia, and a reference allele, conventionally numbered one, represents the fully functional form against which others are described. Each allele is assigned a functional status such as normal, decreased, no function, increased or uncertain, and a person's diplotype, the pair of alleles inherited, is translated into a predicted phenotype. Several practical difficulties follow from the haplotype basis. Short-read genotyping of unphased variants can be ambiguous when two variants could lie on the same or opposite chromosomes, so laboratories infer the most probable diplotype and may report alternatives. Assays detect only the alleles they target, so an untested allele is silently reported as the reference, and this failure mode disproportionately affects people whose ancestry is poorly represented in panel design. Definitions and function assignments are also periodically revised.

Practical example

A laboratory genotypes a metabolism gene and detects the variants defining one named allele on one chromosome and none of the panel's variants on the other. The second chromosome is reported as the reference allele by default. The report states explicitly that this assignment reflects the absence of tested variants, not sequencing of the entire gene.

Common misunderstanding

The asterisk notation suggests that each star allele is a single mutation. Each name denotes a haplotype, sometimes comprising many variants and occasionally a structural change such as a gene deletion or duplication. Reporting a reference allele also means only that no tested variant was found, which is not the same as confirming full function.
