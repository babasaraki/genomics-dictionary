N50

Bioinformatics and statistics Published

Definition

N50 is a summary statistic describing how contiguous a genome assembly is. Sort the assembled pieces from longest to shortest and add their lengths until half the total assembly length is reached; the length of the piece that takes the running total past that halfway point is the N50. A larger N50 means the assembly is built from longer pieces.

Technical explanation

N50 is length-weighted rather than an average: it is the length such that pieces of that size or longer account for at least half the assembly. It is reported for contigs and separately for scaffolds, which include gaps, so the two values are not interchangeable. Related statistics qualify it. NG50 uses the expected genome size as the denominator instead of the assembly length, which prevents an inflated or contaminated assembly from scoring well. L50 counts how many pieces are needed to reach the halfway point. NGA50 is computed after aligning to a trusted reference and breaking the assembly at misjoins, so it penalises contiguity bought at the cost of accuracy. The core limitation is that N50 measures only contiguity, never correctness or completeness. An assembler that aggressively joins pieces across repeats can produce a high N50 with chimeric contigs, and an assembly missing a third of the genome can have an excellent N50 across what remains. Gene completeness and misassembly measures must be reported alongside it.

Practical example

Two assemblers are compared on the same long-read dataset for a bacterial isolate. One returns a higher contig N50, but aligning both assemblies against a closed reference shows that its longest contig joins two regions in the wrong orientation. The team selects the lower-N50 assembly, which is fragmented but free of misjoins, for resistance gene screening.

Common misunderstanding

A higher N50 is widely read as a better assembly. It describes only the length distribution of the pieces. An assembly can achieve a large N50 by incorrectly merging across repeats, or by omitting hard regions altogether. Contiguity, completeness and accuracy are three separate properties and only the first is measured here.
