Effective population size

Ne

Human and population genomics Bioinformatics and statistics Published

Definition

Effective population size, written Ne, is the size of an idealised population that would lose genetic variation at the same rate as the real population being studied. It is usually much smaller than the number of people alive, because real populations have unequal family sizes, overlapping generations and uneven sex ratios. It is a bookkeeping device for predicting drift, not a headcount.

Technical explanation

Effective population size is defined by matching a real population to a Wright-Fisher ideal with respect to a chosen property, which is why several versions exist: inbreeding, variance and coalescent effective sizes need not agree. Ne governs the pace of drift, the amount of neutral diversity maintained at mutation-drift balance, the decay of linkage disequilibrium with distance, and the threshold at which selection outcompetes chance. In human genomics it is estimated from LD patterns, from runs of homozygosity, or from coalescent methods that infer historical trajectories from the distribution of segment lengths within one or a few genomes. Such estimates behave like harmonic means over long spans, so a single bottleneck leaves a lasting imprint. African populations typically show larger long-term effective sizes, and correspondingly greater diversity and shorter-range LD, than populations descended from groups that migrated out of Africa. All estimates are model-dependent, since structure, migration and selection can bias inferred trajectories, so these curves are best read as summaries rather than census histories.

Practical example

A population genomics group infers historical effective size from whole-genome sequences of a small number of individuals. The curve shows a sustained reduction tens of thousands of years ago followed by recent expansion. The group reports the trajectory with explicit caveats, because unmodelled population structure can produce a dip that mimics a true bottleneck.

Common misunderstanding

Effective size is routinely mistaken for the number of people in a population. It is an abstraction describing how fast a population loses variation by chance, and it can be orders of magnitude below the census count. A large modern population can still carry the genetic signature of a small effective size in its past.
