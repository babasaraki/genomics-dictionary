Library preparation

Sequencing and laboratory methods Published

Definition

Library preparation is the set of laboratory steps that turns extracted DNA or RNA into a form a sequencing instrument can read. The material is cut or copied into fragments of suitable length, short known sequences called adapters are attached to the ends, and sample-specific barcodes are added. The quality of this step largely determines the quality of the data.

Technical explanation

Library preparation converts purified nucleic acid into adapter-flanked fragments of a defined size distribution with the sequences required for binding, priming and indexing. Typical steps are fragmentation by sonication, enzymatic digestion or tagmentation; end repair and adenylation where ligation chemistry demands it; adapter ligation or addition during a tagmentation reaction; optional amplification; and size selection by beads or gel. RNA workflows add reverse transcription and some form of selection, commonly polyadenylated transcript capture or ribosomal RNA depletion, each of which changes which transcripts are represented. Target enrichment by hybridisation capture or multiplex amplification is inserted here when only part of the genome is wanted. The protocol determines what the data can show: insert size sets the span of paired reads, amplification cycles set the duplicate rate and composition bias, and fragmentation chemistry introduces its own position preferences. Low-input and degraded samples amplify these effects, since fewer starting molecules mean that stochastic loss of templates, rather than sequencing error, limits what can be detected.

Practical example

A laboratory processing degraded archival tissue finds that libraries from those samples carry far more duplicate reads than libraries from fresh blood on the same run. The cause is the small number of intact starting molecules, which are then amplified heavily. The team reduces amplification cycles and raises the input amount where material allows, instead of simply sequencing deeper.

Common misunderstanding

Library preparation is often seen as routine sample handling with the real measurement happening on the instrument. In reality most of the bias in a sequencing dataset is introduced before loading: fragment size, amplification, enrichment chemistry and the RNA selection method all decide which molecules are present to be read at all.
