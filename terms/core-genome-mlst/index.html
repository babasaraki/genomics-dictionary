Core genome MLST

cgMLST

Pathogen genomics Published

Definition

Core genome multilocus sequence typing, abbreviated cgMLST, extends the idea of typing by gene alleles from a handful of genes to the hundreds or thousands of genes shared by almost all members of a species. Each gene is given an allele number, and two isolates are compared by counting how many of those numbers differ. The result is a portable, high-resolution measure of relatedness.

Technical explanation

Core genome MLST defines a fixed scheme of loci present in nearly all members of a species, assembles each isolate's genome, and calls an allele at every locus against a curated nomenclature server. Distance between isolates is the number of loci with differing alleles, and clustering thresholds are set per species from studies of known transmission and of within-host diversity. Compared with mapping-based single nucleotide variant analysis, allele-based typing is less sensitive to reference choice and to recombination, since a recombined locus counts once regardless of how many substitutions it introduced, and the integer output is readily shared between laboratories without exchanging read data. Its requirements are also stricter: assemblies must be of good quality, because contamination, fragmented contigs or low coverage produce missing or spurious alleles, and loci with missing calls must be handled consistently when distances are computed. Thresholds are guides, not proof of transmission, and they vary with the time elapsed between samples. Scheme versioning matters, as results computed under different scheme or software versions are not directly comparable.

Practical example

A national food safety laboratory types Listeria monocytogenes isolates from clinical cases and from a production environment. Several clinical isolates and one environmental isolate differ by only a few alleles, well inside the species threshold used by the programme, while other clinical isolates are hundreds of alleles away. The close group becomes the focus of a joint investigation with the regulator.

Common misunderstanding

Allele distances are often read as a direct count of mutations. They are not. A single recombination event replacing a stretch of sequence changes one allele, just as a single point mutation does, so the measures differ systematically. Allele thresholds also describe populations, and an isolate just inside or just outside a threshold is not thereby proven linked or unlinked.
