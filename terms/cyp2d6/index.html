CYP2D6

Pharmacogenomics Published

Definition

CYP2D6 is a liver enzyme that processes a substantial share of commonly used medicines, including some painkillers, antidepressants and heart medicines. The gene encoding it is unusually variable: people can carry versions with no activity, reduced activity, or extra copies that increase activity. This variability makes it one of the most studied pharmacogenes.

Technical explanation

The CYP2D6 locus is technically demanding because it sits next to highly similar pseudogenes, which gives rise to deletions, duplications and multiplications of the whole gene and to hybrid genes that combine segments of gene and pseudogene. Accurate genotyping therefore requires copy-number assessment as well as variant detection, and short-read data alone often cannot resolve hybrid structures or assign duplicated copies to a specific allele; long-read sequencing and dedicated assays improve resolution. Allele frequencies differ markedly between genetic ancestry groups, and several reduced-function alleles that are common in African and Asian populations were under-represented in early panels, which caused systematic misclassification of individuals from those populations. Interpretation also depends on the medicine: for an active drug, reduced metabolism raises exposure, whereas for a prodrug that CYP2D6 converts to its active form, reduced metabolism lowers the active concentration. Concomitant inhibitors are common and frequently shift the observed phenotype away from the genotype-based prediction.

Practical example

A laboratory reports two no-function alleles for this gene in a sample, together with a copy-number result showing no duplication. A clinical team interpreting the report consults published gene-drug guidance for the specific medicine concerned, noting that the implication differs depending on whether the medicine is administered in active form or requires conversion by the enzyme.

Common misunderstanding

Readers often assume a single genotype result captures this gene fully. Structural variation means copy number must be measured as well, and panels miss alleles they were not designed to detect, which has affected under-represented populations most. A predicted phenotype can also be overridden by an interacting medicine taken at the same time.
