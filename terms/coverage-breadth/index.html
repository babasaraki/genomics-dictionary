Breadth of coverage

Sequencing and laboratory methods Published

Definition

Breadth of coverage is the proportion of the target region that was read at all, or that reached a stated minimum depth. It answers a different question from read depth: not how many times a position was read, but how much of the intended region was covered. It is usually reported as a percentage of target bases.

Technical explanation

Breadth of coverage quantifies completeness of a sequencing experiment against a defined target, whether that target is a whole genome, an exome, a panel or a pathogen reference. It is normally expressed as the fraction of target bases reaching a threshold depth, for instance the proportion of panel bases covered at least twenty-fold, since breadth without a depth qualifier is ambiguous. Breadth and depth are independent axes: a shallow whole-genome run can have excellent breadth and poor depth, while a deep amplicon panel has the reverse. In pathogen genomics breadth drives whether a consensus genome can be called at all, and incomplete breadth produces stretches of ambiguous bases that affect lineage assignment and phylogenetic placement. Breadth is limited by capture or amplicon design, by sample quality and input quantity, and by mappability, because reads from repetitive or paralogous sequence cannot be placed uniquely. Reporting breadth honestly means stating the target definition used, since the same data give different percentages against a coding region, a padded panel or a whole genome.

Practical example

A sequencing facility processing viral samples with varying amounts of material reports, for each sample, the percentage of the reference genome covered at the laboratory's minimum depth. Samples falling below the stated threshold are flagged as partial genomes and excluded from lineage assignment, rather than being reported with long runs of ambiguous bases.

Common misunderstanding

Coverage is frequently quoted as a single number without saying whether depth or breadth is meant, and the two are sometimes used interchangeably. They are not the same. Thirty-fold depth says how intensively covered bases were read; ninety-five per cent breadth says how much of the target was reached. A result needs both figures to be interpretable.
