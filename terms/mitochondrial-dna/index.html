Mitochondrial DNA

mtDNA

Human and population genomics Genetics foundations Published

Definition

Mitochondrial DNA, or mtDNA, is the small circular genome inside mitochondria, the compartments that generate most of a cell's usable energy. It is separate from the chromosomes in the nucleus, present in many copies per cell, and in humans it is inherited from the mother. It carries a small number of genes, all serving energy production or the machinery that builds mitochondrial proteins.

Technical explanation

The human mitochondrial genome is roughly 16.6 kilobases and encodes thirteen proteins of the oxidative phosphorylation machinery, two ribosomal RNAs and twenty-two transfer RNAs; the great majority of mitochondrial proteins are encoded in the nucleus and imported. Inheritance is effectively maternal and non-recombining, so variants accumulate sequentially along maternal lineages, which is the basis of mitochondrial haplogroup classification and of its use in population history and forensic comparison. A cell holds many mtDNA copies, so a variant may be present in all of them, termed homoplasmy, or in a fraction, termed heteroplasmy. Heteroplasmy levels can differ between tissues and shift between generations through a germline bottleneck, which complicates both diagnosis and prediction. Mitochondrial disease can arise from mtDNA variants or from nuclear genes affecting mitochondrial function, so inheritance patterns differ between conditions. Technically, high copy number makes mtDNA easy to sequence deeply, but nuclear mitochondrial insertions can masquerade as low-level heteroplasmy when alignment is not handled carefully.

Practical example

A laboratory sequencing mitochondrial DNA at high depth detects a variant present in about ten per cent of reads from a blood sample. Because heteroplasmy levels differ between tissues, the result is reported as tissue-specific. The team also checks that the supporting reads do not align equally well to a known nuclear mitochondrial insertion elsewhere in the genome.

Common misunderstanding

Mitochondrial DNA is often said to prove that everyone descends from a single maternal ancestor, or to be inherited only by daughters. It traces one lineage among the many that contribute to any genome, and both sons and daughters inherit their mother's mtDNA, although only daughters pass it on. It also represents a tiny fraction of a person's total DNA.
