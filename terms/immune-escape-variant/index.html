Immune escape variant

Pathogen genomics Published

Definition

An immune escape variant is a version of a pathogen carrying changes that make it less easily recognised by antibodies produced after infection or vaccination. The changes usually sit in the parts of a surface protein that antibodies bind. Escape is partial and relative: it reduces the protection a population has built up rather than abolishing immunity.

Technical explanation

Immune escape arises when variation in antigenic regions reduces binding by neutralising antibodies, or less commonly alters recognition by T cells through changes in presented peptides. Escape variants are detected in several complementary ways: by monitoring substitutions at known antigenic sites during genomic surveillance, by neutralisation assays measuring the reduction in antibody titre against the variant relative to an earlier strain, and by epidemiological studies of reinfection or vaccine effectiveness. Convergent evolution is characteristic, with the same substitutions appearing independently in unrelated lineages, which is itself evidence of selection. Several cautions apply. Reduced neutralisation in a laboratory assay does not translate directly into reduced clinical protection, because cellular immunity and non-neutralising functions contribute to protection against severe disease. Growth advantage of a lineage may reflect higher intrinsic transmissibility, population immunity, behaviour or founder effects rather than escape alone, so attributing a rise in cases to escape requires more than sequence data. Terminology also matters: a variant is any sequence difference, and only a subset has any phenotypic consequence.

Practical example

During routine surveillance of a seasonal respiratory virus, analysts notice that substitutions at the same two antigenic positions have arisen in three distinct lineages within a few months. Independent emergence suggests selection rather than chance. Reference laboratories then perform neutralisation assays with sera from recently vaccinated donors to quantify any reduction in antibody binding.

Common misunderstanding

The word escape is often read as meaning that immunity has stopped working. Protection is layered and graded: a variant that evades some neutralising antibodies may still be controlled by other parts of the immune response. Equally, a variant that is spreading is not automatically an escape variant, since transmissibility and immunity are separate properties requiring separate evidence.
