Open reading frame

ORF

Genetics foundations Published

Definition

An open reading frame is a stretch of sequence that could be read as a continuous set of three-letter codons without hitting a stop signal. Because sequence can be read in several frames and on either strand, software looks for such stretches when predicting where protein-coding genes might lie. An open reading frame is a candidate for coding, not proof of it.

Technical explanation

An open reading frame is a run of codons bounded by an initiation codon and the first in-frame termination codon, identified in one of six possible frames across the two strands. Open reading frame finding is a basic step in prokaryotic gene prediction, where genes are typically uninterrupted and gene density is high, and it is far less sufficient in eukaryotes because introns break coding sequence across exons. Length thresholds reduce spurious predictions, since short open reading frames occur frequently by chance in any sequence, but thresholds also discard genuine short coding sequences, a class increasingly recognised as functional. Supporting evidence from homology, transcript data, ribosome profiling and proteomics is therefore used to promote a predicted open reading frame to an annotated gene. In clinical variant work the concept appears when assessing frameshifts, since an insertion or deletion that is not a multiple of three shifts the frame and usually introduces a premature stop. Note that the abbreviation ORF is also used informally for the predicted protein product itself.

Practical example

A microbiologist assembling a bacterial genome runs gene prediction and obtains thousands of predicted open reading frames. Many short predictions lack any database match or transcript support, so the annotation pipeline flags them as hypothetical rather than asserting function, and the team reports only well-supported genes in the manuscript.

Common misunderstanding

An open reading frame is often taken as equivalent to a gene. It is a sequence pattern that may or may not be transcribed and translated, and in eukaryotes genuine coding sequence is usually split across exons so that no single genomic open reading frame spans the gene. Experimental support is what turns a prediction into an annotation.
