Pathogenic variant

Clinical and cancer genomics Published

Definition

A pathogenic variant is a DNA change that the available evidence shows is capable of causing or strongly predisposing to a disease. It is a judgement about evidence, not a separate kind of molecule: the same sequence change is simply better supported as harmful than the many variants that make no difference. Classifications can be revised when new evidence arrives.

Technical explanation

Pathogenic is the strongest of the classification tiers used for clinical sequence variants, applied when converging lines of evidence support a disease-causing role. Those lines typically include rarity or absence in large population datasets, a predicted effect such as premature termination in a gene where loss of function is an established mechanism, segregation with disease in families, de novo occurrence, case-control data and functional assays. Classification is gene- and disease-specific: the same change may be pathogenic for one condition and irrelevant to another, and expert panels curate gene-specific rules because generic thresholds perform poorly. Importantly, pathogenic describes the variant's capacity to cause disease, not the certainty that an individual will become unwell; penetrance and expressivity determine what is actually observed. Classifications are provisional. Variants are reclassified in both directions as population reference datasets grow and as functional data accumulate, and laboratories therefore record the evidence and the date of assessment so that a report can be revisited rather than treated as permanent.

Practical example

A diagnostic laboratory reviews a nonsense variant in a gene in which loss of function is a well-established disease mechanism. The variant is absent from large population reference datasets, has been submitted to a public variant archive by several laboratories with concordant interpretations, and tracks with the condition across two branches of a family. The laboratory classifies it as pathogenic and records the supporting evidence and assessment date.

Common misunderstanding

Readers often treat variant and pathogenic variant as interchangeable, so that any sequence difference sounds alarming. Every person carries millions of variants, and almost all are benign or of no measurable consequence. Pathogenic is a conclusion reached only after specific evidence has been weighed, and it can be withdrawn if that evidence changes.
