Promoter

Genetics foundations Published

Definition

A promoter is a stretch of DNA just before a gene where the machinery that makes RNA first assembles. It acts as a starting signal, setting where transcription begins and influencing how often it happens. Promoters contain short sequence patterns recognised by proteins, and their activity can differ between tissues and between conditions within the same organism.

Technical explanation

A promoter is the regulatory region surrounding a transcription start site at which general transcription factors and RNA polymerase assemble. Core promoter elements provide positioning signals, while proximal and distal elements, including enhancers acting over long distances through chromatin looping, set the level of transcription. Many human promoters lie in CpG islands that are typically unmethylated when the associated gene is active, and aberrant promoter methylation is a recognised mechanism of gene silencing, including silencing of tumour suppressor genes in cancer. Promoter usage is not fixed per gene: alternative promoters generate transcripts with different first exons and sometimes different protein products or tissue distributions. Experimentally, promoter activity is assessed by reporter assays, accessibility assays and start-site mapping, each measuring a different proxy. A limitation for variant interpretation is that non-coding promoter variants are difficult to classify, because evidence frameworks were built around coding consequence and functional data for individual regulatory positions are usually absent.

Practical example

An analyst investigating an unsolved case finds no coding variant but notes a rare change in the promoter of a strong candidate gene. Without functional data or recurrent observations in similarly affected individuals, the finding remains of uncertain significance, and the laboratory records it as a research observation rather than a diagnostic result.

Common misunderstanding

Promoters are often imagined as simple on switches sitting immediately before every gene. Transcriptional control is distributed across promoters, enhancers, insulators and chromatin state, many genes have several promoters, and promoter activity varies by cell type. A variant in a promoter is therefore not automatically consequential, nor automatically harmless.
