Nagoya Protocol

Ethics and data governance Published

Definition

The Nagoya Protocol is an international agreement under the Convention on Biological Diversity covering access to genetic resources and the sharing of benefits arising from their use. Countries that have joined it require users to obtain permission before collecting biological material and to agree terms for sharing benefits. Many research projects involving samples collected abroad must comply.

Technical explanation

The Nagoya Protocol operationalises the third objective of the Convention on Biological Diversity by establishing that access to genetic resources requires prior informed consent of the providing country and mutually agreed terms governing benefit sharing, with compliance monitored through national focal points, competent authorities and internationally recognised certificates. Implementation is national, so requirements, permit procedures and timelines differ considerably between countries, and researchers must check the law of the country of collection rather than assume a common standard. Scope is a live question in genomics. Human genetic resources fall outside the Convention, but pathogens, vectors, animals, plants and microbial isolates are covered, which directly affects pathogen genomics, agricultural genomics and biodiversity work. Whether digital sequence information derived from a physical sample attracts the same obligations has been the subject of prolonged international negotiation, with implications for open sequence databases and for rapid outbreak data sharing. Practical compliance involves permits obtained before collection, documented terms, tracking of derivatives and attention to how downstream recipients are bound, which is usually handled through material transfer agreements.

Practical example

A team planning to sequence mosquito vectors collected in a partner country applies for an access permit and negotiates terms before fieldwork, covering publication, deposition of sequences and training for the partner institution. The agreement specifies how derived data may be reused by third parties, so that later deposition in a public archive does not conflict with the terms.

Common misunderstanding

Researchers frequently assume the Protocol concerns only commercial bioprospecting. Non-commercial academic research is covered in many jurisdictions, and permits often cannot be obtained retrospectively, which can prevent publication or deposition of data. A second misconception is that it governs human samples; human genetic resources sit outside it, though other national laws and ethics requirements still apply.
