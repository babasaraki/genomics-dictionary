Differential expression

Bioinformatics and statistics Published

Definition

Differential expression analysis asks which genes or transcripts are expressed at different levels between conditions, such as treated and untreated cells, or tumour and adjacent tissue. It compares normalised measurements across replicate samples, estimates how much each feature changes, and assesses whether the change is larger than the variability seen between replicates of the same condition.

Technical explanation

Counts from RNA sequencing are modelled with the negative binomial distribution, which accommodates the overdispersion seen in biological replicates, or transformed and analysed with linear models that weight observations by their precision. Because sample sizes are small and features are numerous, established tools share information across genes to stabilise dispersion estimates, and shrink fold-change estimates for weakly expressed features so that the gene list is not dominated by noisy low-count entries. Results combine an effect size with an adjusted p-value controlling the false discovery rate across all features tested. Several constraints shape interpretation. Statistical power depends on biological replicates, not on sequencing depth beyond a modest point, and technical replicates do not substitute for them. Bulk measurements average across the cells in a tissue, so an apparent change may reflect a shift in cellular composition rather than regulation within any cell type. Transcript abundance is also an imperfect proxy for protein abundance and for activity. Finally, the analysis locates correlates of a condition, not causes of it.

Practical example

A team compares bladder tumour tissue with adjacent non-tumour tissue from the same donors and finds several hundred genes with adjusted p-values below five per cent. Because tumour samples contain more immune cells, they estimate cell-type proportions and re-analyse with those proportions as covariates, which removes many of the immune-related genes from the list.

Common misunderstanding

A long list of significant genes is often read as a list of genes driving the condition. Differential expression describes association between transcript level and group, measured at one time point in a mixture of cell types. Causal direction is unresolved, and many changes are downstream consequences rather than drivers of the biology.
