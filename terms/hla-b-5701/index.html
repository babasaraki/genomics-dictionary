HLA-B*57:01

Pharmacogenomics Published

Definition

HLA-B*57:01 is one version of an immune system gene that presents fragments of proteins to T cells. People carrying this version are at high risk of a serious hypersensitivity reaction to the antiretroviral medicine abacavir. Testing for it before the medicine is started is one of the earliest and clearest examples of pharmacogenomics in routine practice.

Technical explanation

The HLA class I locus is extremely polymorphic, and alleles are named by a standard nomenclature in which the digits before the colon denote the allele group and those after it denote the specific protein sequence. The mechanism of abacavir hypersensitivity is now well characterised: the drug binds non-covalently within the peptide-binding groove of the HLA-B*57:01 molecule, altering the repertoire of self peptides presented and provoking a T cell response against tissues that would otherwise be ignored. Prospective screening before prescribing was shown in a randomised trial to abolish immunologically confirmed hypersensitivity, and it has been adopted widely. Testing is by targeted genotyping, sequence-based typing or tag approaches, and the negative predictive value is high while the positive predictive value is lower, since not every carrier would have reacted. Allele frequency differs between populations, which affects the number needed to test but not the strength of the association. Related HLA associations exist for other medicines, with different alleles and different populations involved.

Practical example

A clinic adopts routine typing for this allele before prescribing abacavir. Results are recorded in the patient's record as a persistent flag rather than as a one-off laboratory result, because the genotype does not change and may be relevant to later prescribing decisions. Patients who test positive are offered an alternative regimen chosen by the treating clinician.

Common misunderstanding

A positive result is often read as meaning the reaction has already begun or is certain to occur. It identifies susceptibility before exposure, and not every carrier would develop hypersensitivity. Conversely, the allele predicts this specific drug reaction and is not a general marker of allergy or of risk from unrelated medicines.
