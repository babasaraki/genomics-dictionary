Penetrance

Clinical and cancer genomics Published

Definition

Penetrance is the proportion of people carrying a particular genotype who actually show the associated trait or condition. If every carrier is affected, penetrance is complete; if only some are, it is reduced or incomplete. Penetrance is a property of a variant in a population and a context, not a prediction about any single person.

Technical explanation

Penetrance is usually expressed as the proportion of carriers affected by a given age, since many conditions have age-dependent onset and a lifetime figure conceals the trajectory. Estimates depend heavily on how carriers were identified. Figures derived from families referred to clinics because several members are affected are systematically higher than those derived from unselected population cohorts or biobanks, a bias that has prompted substantial downward revision of some earlier estimates. Penetrance is modified by other variants in the genome, by polygenic background, by sex, by age and by environmental and lifestyle exposures, so a single number for a gene is at best an average over a studied population. Reduced penetrance also complicates variant interpretation, because a pathogenic variant may appear in an unaffected parent without contradicting its classification. For risk communication, intervals and the ascertainment context matter more than a point estimate, and penetrance estimated in one population should not be assumed to transfer unchanged to another.

Practical example

A research team estimates lifetime risk for a cancer-susceptibility gene first from families attending genetics clinics and then from an unselected biobank cohort. The clinic-based estimate is appreciably higher, because those families were referred precisely because the condition clustered in them. The team reports both, with intervals, and states clearly how carriers were ascertained in each setting.

Common misunderstanding

Carrying a pathogenic variant is often heard as a diagnosis in waiting. For most conditions penetrance is incomplete, so some carriers never develop the condition at all. Conversely, a variant with reduced penetrance that shows up in a healthy relative has not been disproved; absence of the trait in one carrier is expected, not contradictory.
