Meiosis

Genetics foundations Published

Definition

Meiosis is the specialised form of cell division that produces eggs and sperm. It halves the number of chromosomes so that each gamete carries one copy of each chromosome instead of two. When egg and sperm combine, the full number is restored. Meiosis also shuffles the genetic material inherited from each parent, so siblings are genetically different.

Technical explanation

Meiosis consists of one round of DNA replication followed by two divisions. In prophase of the first division, homologous chromosomes pair and exchange segments by recombination at crossover sites, producing chromatids that combine maternal and paternal sequence. Independent assortment of homologues at the first division and separation of sister chromatids at the second generate haploid cells whose combinations are effectively unique. Errors in segregation produce gametes with extra or missing chromosomes, the basis of aneuploidy, and the risk of certain segregation errors increases with maternal age. Recombination is not uniform along chromosomes: rates concentrate in hotspots, and the resulting pattern of linkage disequilibrium is what makes genome-wide association and imputation possible. Meiotic recombination also occasionally produces structural rearrangements through non-allelic homologous recombination between repeated sequences, a recognised mechanism for recurrent copy-number variants. Understanding meiosis underpins the interpretation of inheritance patterns and of population genetic structure.

Practical example

A cytogeneticist reviewing a prenatal result sees an extra copy of a chromosome. The most common origin of such an imbalance is a segregation error during the formation of a gamete, and explaining this origin helps a counselling team describe why the finding usually does not indicate anything the parents did or could have prevented.

Common misunderstanding

Meiosis is often confused with mitosis. Mitosis produces two genetically identical cells for growth and repair, whereas meiosis produces four genetically distinct haploid cells for reproduction. Another misconception is that a chromosome passed on is a single parental chromosome; recombination means most transmitted chromosomes are mosaics of both grandparental copies.
