CYP2C19

Pharmacogenomics Published

Definition

CYP2C19 is a liver enzyme involved in processing several widely used medicines, including some that reduce stomach acid, some antidepressants and a drug that prevents blood clots by being converted into its active form. Inherited versions of the gene range from no activity to increased activity, which alters how much active drug a person ends up with.

Technical explanation

CYP2C19 variation is dominated by a small number of defined haplotypes: loss-of-function alleles caused by splice-disrupting and premature termination variants, and an increased-function promoter allele that raises transcription. Unlike CYP2D6, structural variation is not a major feature, so genotyping is technically simpler, though panels still detect only the alleles they target and rarer loss-of-function alleles are missed unless included. Allele frequencies vary considerably between populations, with loss-of-function alleles reaching high frequency in several East and South-East Asian populations, so assay design and expected phenotype distributions should not be generalised from one population to another. The direction of clinical effect depends on the medicine: reduced activity lowers formation of the active metabolite of a prodrug, while it raises exposure to drugs cleared by the enzyme. Proton pump inhibitors, which both act as substrates and inhibit the enzyme, illustrate how interacting medicines can shift the observed phenotype away from the genotype prediction.

Practical example

A cardiology service receives a pharmacogenomic report describing a patient as a predicted poor metaboliser for this enzyme, in the context of a medicine that requires enzymatic conversion to become active. The team consults the relevant published guideline and records the genotype, the predicted phenotype and the assay's allele coverage in the patient's notes for future prescribing decisions.

Common misunderstanding

A poor metaboliser result is sometimes read as meaning every medicine will build up to dangerous levels. The consequence depends entirely on the medicine: for a prodrug, less active compound is formed, which is the opposite concern. The result also applies only to pathways in which this particular enzyme plays a substantial role.
