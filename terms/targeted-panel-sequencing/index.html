Targeted panel sequencing

Sequencing and laboratory methods Published

Definition

Targeted panel sequencing reads only a chosen set of genes or regions instead of the whole genome. The regions are captured or amplified before sequencing, so most of the data come from the targets. Concentrating the reading effort on a small target allows much greater depth for the same cost, which matters when looking for rare or low-level variants.

Technical explanation

Panels enrich a defined target before or during library preparation, either by hybridisation capture with biotinylated probes or by multiplex amplification of the target regions. Enrichment allows depth that would be unaffordable genome-wide, which is why panels dominate somatic tumour testing, cell-free DNA assays and pathogen resistance typing, where detecting variants at low allele fraction is the point. A panel is a deliberate restriction: it can only report on content within its design, so its gene list, exon boundaries and padding define its clinical scope, and a negative result means no reportable variant was found in those regions. Enrichment also introduces its own behaviour. Capture efficiency varies by probe, leaving some exons reliably shallow; amplicon designs are vulnerable to variants under primer sites and generally detect copy-number and structural changes poorly; and both approaches require validation of each region against a reference material. Panels age as knowledge changes, so the design, its version and its measured per-base coverage should accompany any result.

Practical example

A molecular pathology laboratory uses a solid tumour panel covering a defined set of genes at high depth to look for somatic variants in a biopsy. A clinician asks whether a gene outside the panel was altered. The laboratory explains that this gene was never interrogated, so the result is silent about it rather than negative for it.

Common misunderstanding

A negative panel result is frequently read as the absence of any relevant variant. It means only that no reportable variant was detected within the regions the panel covers, at the depth achieved. Genes outside the design, intronic and regulatory sequence, and variant types the chemistry detects poorly, such as large rearrangements, are simply not examined.
