Variant

Genetics foundations Published

Definition

A variant is a difference between the DNA sequence observed in a sample and the sequence of the reference used for comparison. Variants are ordinary: every person carries millions of them. A variant is simply a difference, and saying that one exists tells you nothing about whether it affects health. That judgement requires separate evidence and a formal classification process.

Technical explanation

Variant is the preferred neutral term for an observed sequence difference, replacing earlier usage in which mutation implied harm and polymorphism implied benignity by frequency alone. Variants are described against a named reference assembly and transcript using standard nomenclature, and they span classes from single-nucleotide changes and small insertions or deletions to structural and copy-number events. Clinical classification under consensus frameworks weighs population frequency, computational prediction, functional data, segregation in families and case-control evidence to place a variant into categories from benign to pathogenic, with uncertain significance used when evidence is insufficient. Classifications are provisional and are revised as evidence accumulates, so laboratories operate reinterpretation policies. Frequency data are population-specific, and because reference datasets under-represent many populations, variants common in those groups have historically been over-called as candidate disease variants. Detection also varies by class and technology: structural and repeat-expansion variants are poorly captured by standard short-read pipelines, so absence of a reported variant is not evidence that none exists.

Practical example

A laboratory reviewing an older report finds a variant previously classified as uncertain. Larger and more diverse population datasets now show it at appreciable frequency in an ancestry group that was barely represented when the original assessment was made, and the classification is revised accordingly and reissued.

Common misunderstanding

The word variant is often read as a synonym for something wrong. A variant is a difference from a reference sequence, and the reference is a convention rather than a standard of health. It is equally mistaken to assume a variant classification is permanent; classifications reflect current evidence and are expected to change.
