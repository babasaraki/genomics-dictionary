Haplotype phasing

Human and population genomics Bioinformatics and statistics Published

Definition

Haplotype phasing works out which of a person's genetic variants sit on the same copy of a chromosome. Standard genotyping records that someone carries two different versions at two positions, but not how those versions are paired between the maternal and paternal copies. Phasing resolves that arrangement, either by statistical comparison with known haplotypes or by reading long DNA molecules directly.

Technical explanation

Phasing converts unphased genotypes into two haplotypes per individual. Statistical phasing uses hidden Markov models to match an individual's genotypes against a reference panel of haplotypes, with accuracy reported as a switch error rate; it is efficient for common variants in well-represented populations but degrades for rare variants and for samples whose ancestry is poorly covered by the panel. Pedigree phasing uses transmitted alleles in trios and is highly accurate but requires family samples. Read-based or molecular phasing links variants observed on the same long read, linked-read barcode or chromatin contact, and resolves rare and de novo variants that statistical methods cannot, with phase block length limited by read length and by the spacing of heterozygous sites. Hybrid approaches combine the two. Phase matters for compound heterozygosity in recessive disease, for pharmacogenomic star allele assignment, for HLA typing, for distinguishing cis from trans regulatory effects, and for haplotype-resolved genome assembly. The caveat is that phased output always carries uncertainty that downstream tools frequently discard.

Practical example

A diagnostic laboratory finds two different loss-of-function variants in the same gene in a person with a suspected recessive condition, and no parental samples are available. The team uses long-read sequencing to establish whether the variants lie on the same chromosome copy or on opposite copies, because only the latter arrangement leaves no intact copy of the gene.

Common misunderstanding

Phasing is often assumed to come free with sequencing. A standard short-read variant call file is unphased, and phase must be inferred or generated deliberately. Statistical phase is also not a certainty: switch errors occur, most often around rare variants, so treating a phased call as a direct observation can support confident but wrong conclusions.
