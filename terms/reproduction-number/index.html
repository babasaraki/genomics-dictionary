Reproduction number

R0

Pathogen genomics Published

Definition

The reproduction number is the average number of new infections caused by one infected person. The basic reproduction number, written R0, applies to a fully susceptible population, while the effective reproduction number applies to the population as it actually is, with immunity and control measures in place. A value above one means the epidemic is growing.

Technical explanation

The basic reproduction number is a theoretical quantity for a wholly susceptible, homogeneously mixing population, determined by transmissibility, contact rates and infectious duration. The effective reproduction number, often written with a time subscript, is the quantity surveillance actually estimates, reflecting accumulated immunity, behaviour and interventions. Estimation methods include fitting compartmental models, renewal equation approaches that combine the time series of cases with an assumed generation interval distribution, and phylodynamic birth-death models that infer the ratio of transmission to removal from sequence data and sampling times. Each approach is sensitive to its inputs: case-based estimates depend on testing rates and reporting delays, while sequence-based estimates depend on sampling intensity and on the molecular clock. Averages also conceal heterogeneity, since many infections produce no onward transmission while a minority produce many, so the same mean is compatible with very different epidemic behaviour. Estimates are population-specific and time-specific; quoting a single value for a pathogen without stating the setting, period and method is not meaningful.

Practical example

An analyst preparing a weekly situation report estimates the effective reproduction number from reported cases using a renewal method, and separately from genomic data using a birth-death model. The two estimates overlap in their credible intervals but the genomic estimate is less affected by a change in testing policy that week. Both are reported, with their assumptions stated.

Common misunderstanding

R0 is often quoted as a fixed property of a pathogen, as though it were a constant like a melting point. It depends on contact patterns, population structure and immunity, so it differs between settings and over time. The abbreviation is also used loosely for the effective reproduction number, which is a different quantity and should be labelled separately.
