Passenger mutation

Clinical and cancer genomics Published

Definition

A passenger mutation is a change in a tumour's DNA that happened along the way but does not itself help the tumour grow. Cells accumulate such changes continually, and when one cell gives rise to a tumour, all the changes it had already collected are carried along. Passengers greatly outnumber the changes that actually drive the cancer.

Technical explanation

Passenger mutations arise from the same processes as drivers, including replication errors, endogenous damage, defective repair and exogenous mutagens, but they confer no selective advantage. They are nonetheless informative. Their number and spectrum underpin tumour mutational burden and mutational signature analysis, and because they accumulate neutrally they act as molecular markers for reconstructing the ancestry of tumour subclones. The driver-passenger boundary is not sharp: some alterations are weakly advantageous, some become advantageous only under treatment pressure, and classification depends on the strength of the available evidence, so a variant currently called a passenger may be reclassified. Passengers can also have clinical consequences without driving growth, for example when they generate neoantigens recognised by the immune system, which is one reason that highly mutated tumours may respond to immune checkpoint inhibition. In reporting, passenger variants are usually not actioned, but suppressing them entirely from analysis would discard the signal needed for clonality and signature work.

Practical example

A research team sequences several regions of one tumour and uses the pattern of passenger variants shared between regions to build a tree of the tumour's subclones. Variants present in every region are placed on the trunk; those confined to one region mark later branches. None of these passengers is reported for clinical action, yet they define the tumour's evolutionary history.

Common misunderstanding

Passenger is often taken to mean unimportant and therefore safe to discard. Passengers do not drive growth, but they carry the information used for mutational signatures, tumour mutational burden and clonal reconstruction, and some generate neoantigens. The label describes a lack of growth advantage, not a lack of biological or analytical relevance.
