Cycle threshold

Ct

Sequencing and laboratory methods Published

Definition

The cycle threshold, written Ct, is the number of amplification cycles a quantitative PCR test needs before its fluorescent signal becomes clearly detectable. A sample containing more of the target reaches that point sooner, so a lower cycle threshold indicates more starting material. The value depends on the particular assay and instrument used.

Technical explanation

The cycle threshold, also reported as the quantification cycle, is the fractional cycle number at which amplification signal crosses a defined threshold above background. Under ideal doubling, it is inversely proportional to the logarithm of the initial template concentration, which is the basis of quantification against a standard curve. In practice the value is a property of the whole measurement system: the threshold setting, baseline correction, assay efficiency, target region, reverse transcription step for RNA targets, extraction method and elution volume all shift it. Consequently values are comparable within an assay and laboratory but not between them without calibration to a common standard. In diagnostics the cycle threshold is sometimes used as a proxy for the amount of pathogen material present, and in wastewater surveillance as a relative indicator of community trends, but it reflects the quantity of target nucleic acid in the processed sample rather than infectiousness, viable organism or clinical severity. Sampling quality and the time since onset influence it strongly, so single values carry little interpretive weight.

Practical example

A surveillance programme tracking a pathogen in wastewater reports cycle threshold values over time from the same assay, laboratory and processing protocol. Falling values across consecutive weeks suggest rising target concentration in the catchment. The programme reports the trend rather than individual values, and normalises against a faecal marker to account for dilution by rainfall.

Common misunderstanding

A cycle threshold is often treated as a direct measure of how much pathogen a person carries, or how infectious they are. It measures target nucleic acid recovered from the processed sample in one specific assay. Sampling technique, extraction and assay design all move the number, and nucleic acid detection does not demonstrate viable organism.
