Telomere

Genetics foundations Published

Definition

A telomere is the protective cap at each end of a chromosome, made of a short DNA sequence repeated many times together with bound proteins. Telomeres stop the ends of chromosomes from being treated as broken DNA and prevent them from fusing together. They shorten each time most cells divide, which limits how many divisions a cell can undergo.

Technical explanation

Human telomeres consist of tandem TTAGGG repeats bound by the shelterin complex, which conceals the chromosome end from the DNA damage response and from inappropriate repair. Because conventional replication cannot copy the extreme end of a linear molecule, telomeres shorten with each division in most somatic cells until a critical length triggers senescence or apoptosis. Telomerase, a reverse transcriptase with an RNA template, extends telomeres and is active in germ cells, stem cells and most cancers, while a subset of tumours maintains length by a recombination-based alternative mechanism. Inherited defects in telomere maintenance underlie a recognised group of disorders affecting proliferative tissues. Measurement is technically demanding: repetitive sequence defeats short-read assembly, and quantitative methods differ in what they report, so values are not readily comparable between laboratories. Telomere length in blood correlates weakly with age at population level and is a poor individual predictor, which is why commercial biological age claims should be treated sceptically.

Practical example

A genome assembly team using long reads is able to resolve repetitive terminal sequence that short-read data left as gaps. The resulting contiguous assembly extends into regions that earlier references represented as unknown bases, allowing analysis of sequence that had previously been excluded from variant calling altogether.

Common misunderstanding

Telomere length is often presented as a measure of personal biological age or lifespan. The correlation with chronological age is weak at the individual level, assays vary between laboratories, and no single measurement supports conclusions about an individual's health. Telomere shortening is also not the sole cause of ageing, which involves many interacting processes.
