Recessive inheritance

Genetics foundations Published

Definition

Recessive inheritance describes a pattern in which both copies of a gene must be altered before a trait or condition appears. Someone with one altered copy and one unaltered copy is usually unaffected and is described as a carrier. When both parents are carriers, each child has about a one in four chance of inheriting both altered copies.

Technical explanation

In autosomal recessive conditions, affected individuals carry variants on both copies of a gene, either the same variant twice or two different variants, one on each parental chromosome. Establishing that two variants lie in trans, meaning on opposite copies, is essential, and parental testing or read-based phasing provides this; two variants in cis on the same copy leave an intact gene and do not explain a recessive condition. Carrier frequencies differ between populations because of demographic history including founder effects and consanguinity, so carrier screening panels designed for one population perform differently elsewhere, and panels assembled largely from European data under-detect in other groups. Carriers are generally unaffected but may show measurable biochemical differences, and for some conditions, such as sickle cell trait, the carrier state has recognised physiological consequences under particular circumstances. Pedigrees may show affected siblings without affected parents, and in small families the pattern can be indistinguishable from a sporadic occurrence.

Practical example

A genetics service assessing a child with an inherited metabolic condition finds two variants in the relevant gene. Parental samples show one variant in each parent, confirming they lie on opposite chromosome copies, which supports the diagnosis in a way that the child's result alone could not.

Common misunderstanding

Carriers are often assumed to be at risk of developing the condition themselves. For most recessive conditions a single altered copy does not cause disease, although it is relevant to reproductive planning. A further misunderstanding is that recessive conditions are rare everywhere; carrier frequencies vary between populations, and some are common in particular communities.
