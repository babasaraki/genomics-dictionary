Sensitivity and specificity

Bioinformatics and statistics Clinical and cancer genomics Published

Definition

Sensitivity is the proportion of people or samples that truly have a condition and are correctly identified by a test. Specificity is the proportion of those without the condition that the test correctly calls negative. Both are properties of the test measured against a reference standard, and they describe performance in two different groups, so they must be reported together.

Technical explanation

Sensitivity and specificity are conditional on true status: sensitivity is calculated only among true positives and specificity only among true negatives. Because they condition on the truth rather than on the result, they are relatively stable across settings with different prevalence, which is why they are quoted as test characteristics. They are not independent of each other: moving the threshold of a quantitative assay or a bioinformatic filter trades one against the other, and the receiver operating characteristic curve summarises that trade-off across all thresholds. In genomic analysis the same framework applies to variant calling, where sensitivity is the fraction of true variants recovered and specificity relates to the false positive burden, usually assessed against a well-characterised benchmark sample. Several caveats are essential. Both measures depend on the reference standard, which is itself imperfect. They also depend on the spectrum of cases examined, so a test evaluated on clear-cut samples performs better than it will in routine use. Finally, neither tells a person what a given result means, because that also requires prevalence.

Practical example

A laboratory validating a new variant calling pipeline runs a reference material whose true genotypes are established. The pipeline recovers almost all single-nucleotide variants but misses a notable share of small insertions and deletions. Performance is therefore reported separately by variant class, rather than as one overall figure that would obscure the weakness.

Common misunderstanding

Sensitivity and specificity are often treated as if they told you how likely a positive result is to be right. They do not, because they are calculated in the opposite direction, from true status to result. Translating a result into a probability for the person tested requires the prevalence in the group being tested as well.
