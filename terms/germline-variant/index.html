Germline variant

Clinical and cancer genomics Published

Definition

A germline variant is a difference in the DNA sequence that was present in the egg or sperm cell from which a person developed. Because it was there from the beginning, it is carried in essentially every cell of the body and can be passed on to children. Most germline variants are harmless differences between people; only a small minority affect health.

Technical explanation

Germline variants arise in gametes or in the earliest cell divisions of the zygote, so they are present in reproductive and somatic tissues alike. Clinical laboratories usually detect them from blood, saliva or buccal samples, which sample white blood cells or epithelial cells rather than diseased tissue. Interpretation draws on population allele frequency, computational prediction, segregation within a family and functional data, and the result is issued as a classification rather than a simple present or absent call. Germline variants span the whole range of consequence, from common polymorphisms with no measurable effect, through variants of uncertain significance, to pathogenic variants that substantially raise the risk of a condition. A practical limitation is coverage: short-read assays capture single-nucleotide and small insertion-deletion variants well, but repeat expansions, large structural rearrangements and variants inside segmental duplications are often missed, so a negative germline result narrows the possibilities without excluding a genetic explanation. Mosaic variants acquired after fertilisation can also resemble germline findings when they reach a high allele fraction in blood.

Practical example

A laboratory receives a blood sample from an adult whose family has a striking history of early-onset breast and ovarian cancer. Sequencing of a germline panel finds a frameshift variant in BRCA1 at roughly half of the reads covering that position, consistent with one inherited copy. Because the finding is germline rather than tumour-only, the report notes that blood relatives may carry the same variant and that cascade testing can be discussed.

Common misunderstanding

Many readers assume that germline means disease is certain. It does not. Germline describes only where a variant came from and that it is present throughout the body. The large majority of germline variants are benign, and even a pathogenic germline variant usually raises the chance of a condition rather than guaranteeing it.
