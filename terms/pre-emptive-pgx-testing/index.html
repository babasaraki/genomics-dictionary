Pre-emptive pharmacogenomic testing

Pharmacogenomics Published

Definition

Pre-emptive pharmacogenomic testing means testing a person's relevant genes before any particular medicine is being considered, and storing the result for future use. Because inherited genotypes do not change, one test can inform prescribing decisions for years. The alternative, reactive testing, happens only when a specific medicine is about to be prescribed.

Technical explanation

The case for a pre-emptive model rests on timing and coverage: results are already available at the moment of prescribing, avoiding the delay that discourages reactive testing, and a panel covering several genes can serve many future prescriptions at little extra marginal cost compared with testing one gene at a time. Implementation requires more than a laboratory assay. Results must be stored as structured, computable data in the health record, linked to decision support that fires at prescribing, and presented in a form that non-specialists can act on; a result buried in a scanned report delivers no benefit. Governance questions include consent for future use, how results follow a person between providers, and how a stored interpretation is updated when allele function assignments or guidelines are revised. Evidence on clinical and economic outcomes is accumulating, with trials of multi-gene pre-emptive panels reporting reductions in clinically relevant adverse drug reactions, while cost-effectiveness depends on prescribing patterns and health system structure and therefore varies between settings.

Practical example

A health service pilots a panel covering several pharmacogenes for patients likely to be prescribed relevant medicines. Results are written into the electronic record as structured data linked to prescribing alerts, rather than as a document. When guideline updates change a phenotype assignment, the informatics team re-runs the translation on stored genotypes instead of recalling patients for a new sample.

Common misunderstanding

Pre-emptive testing is sometimes imagined as a one-off that settles prescribing permanently. The genotype is stable, but its interpretation is not: allele function assignments, guidelines and the medicines a person takes all change. Without structured storage and decision support at the point of prescribing, a stored result rarely influences care at all.
