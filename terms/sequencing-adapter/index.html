Sequencing adapter

Sequencing and laboratory methods Published

Definition

A sequencing adapter is a short, known piece of DNA attached to each end of a fragment during library preparation. It is not part of the sample. Adapters give the instrument somewhere to bind the fragment and start reading, and they usually carry the barcode that identifies the sample. Adapter sequence is removed before analysis.

Technical explanation

Adapters are synthetic oligonucleotide constructs ligated or transposed onto library fragments to supply every function the instrument requires outside the insert itself: a sequence complementary to the flow cell or bead, a priming site for the sequencing reaction, and one or two index sequences for demultiplexing. Designs vary, with Y-shaped and hairpin forms used to control which strand is read and to keep the two ends distinguishable. Because adapters are a fixed, known sequence, their presence in the data is diagnostic. When the insert is shorter than the read length, the polymerase reads through the insert into the opposite adapter, and that adapter sequence appears at the end of the read; left in place it will fail to map or, worse, map spuriously. Trimming tools detect and remove these sequences before alignment. Adapters also create artefacts when they self-ligate, producing adapter dimers that consume capacity on the instrument without yielding useful data, which is why size selection and library quality checks look for them specifically.

Practical example

A technologist reviewing a quality report for an RNA library sees adapter sequence detected in a large proportion of reads. The insert size distribution confirms the fragments were shorter than the configured read length, so reads ran off the end of the insert. Trimming the adapters restores normal mapping rates without repeating the run.

Common misunderstanding

Adapters are sometimes mistaken for part of the sequenced organism, especially by newcomers who blast an untrimmed read and find no match. Adapter sequence is added in the laboratory and carries no biological information from the sample. Equally, removing adapters is not optional tidying: untrimmed adapter sequence distorts mapping and downstream quantification.
