Read mapping

Bioinformatics and statistics Published

Definition

Read mapping is the process of finding, for each short sequencing read, the place in a reference genome it most likely came from. Millions of reads are placed in this way, producing a pile of overlapping reads at every covered position. Everything that follows, including variant calling and expression counting, depends on those placements being broadly correct.

Technical explanation

Mappers index the reference once, typically using the Burrows-Wheeler transform or a hash of short seeds, then for each read find candidate positions and extend them into a full alignment. Paired-end information constrains placement, because the two mates should map in the expected orientation and at a plausible distance. Each alignment receives a mapping quality representing the probability that the placement is wrong, which falls to zero where several locations score equally. Spliced aligners are required for RNA, since reads cross exon junctions and must be allowed to skip introns. Mapping is fast and scales well, but it is biased towards the reference: reads carrying large insertions, highly divergent haplotypes or sequence absent from the reference map poorly or not at all, which systematically reduces sensitivity for samples whose ancestry is less well represented in the reference. Graph and pangenome references are being adopted to reduce this bias. Duplicate reads arising from library amplification are usually marked after mapping so that they do not count as independent observations.

Practical example

A team sequencing isolates for a resistance survey finds that one gene appears absent in several samples. Inspection shows the reads are present but unmapped, because the reference strain lacks the plasmid carrying the gene. Assembling the unmapped reads recovers the sequence, and the pipeline is changed to screen assemblies rather than relying on mapping alone.

Common misunderstanding

Unmapped reads are often treated as waste. They are frequently the most informative part of a dataset: they can hold sequence genuinely absent from the reference, contamination, adapter-only fragments or a co-infecting organism. Discarding them without inspection can mean missing a plasmid, an insertion or an unexpected pathogen entirely.
