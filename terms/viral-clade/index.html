Clade

Pathogen genomics Published

Definition

A clade is a group on an evolutionary tree made up of one ancestor and all of its descendants. In pathogen genomics, clade labels give convenient names to broad branches of a virus or bacterium, so that scientists discussing a seasonal influenza clade or a SARS-CoV-2 clade mean the same set of sequences. Clades are nested: a smaller clade sits inside a larger one.

Technical explanation

A clade is a monophyletic group: a node on a phylogeny together with every tip descending from it. Clade nomenclature systems for pathogens select nodes that are useful for communication, usually because they are well supported, globally distributed and persistent, and give them stable labels. Different systems coexist for the same organism, for example Nextstrain clades alongside Pango lineages for SARS-CoV-2, or WHO clades for mpox virus, and these are not interchangeable even though they describe the same underlying tree. A clade is defined by shared derived characters, typically nucleotide substitutions, inherited from the common ancestor. Three cautions apply. Clade membership depends on the inferred tree, which can change with new data, better models or different rooting. Recombination between lineages violates the assumption of a single branching history and can place a genome ambiguously. Finally, a clade is a statement about ancestry only; antigenic or phenotypic similarity within a clade must be measured, not assumed, since convergent changes arise in separate clades under similar selection.

Practical example

An influenza reference laboratory reports that most recent isolates in its region fall into one clade, while a smaller set belongs to a sister clade. Antigenic characterisation is performed separately, because two isolates in the same clade can still differ at key antigenic sites. The combined genetic and antigenic picture is submitted to the international vaccine composition process.

Common misunderstanding

Clade and lineage are often used as if they were one thing. A clade is a defined group on a tree; lineage is used more loosely, sometimes for a clade and sometimes for a line of descent through time. Different naming systems may also label overlapping groups differently, so the system should always be stated with the name.
