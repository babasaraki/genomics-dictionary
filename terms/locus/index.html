Locus

Genetics foundations Published

Definition

A locus is a specific location in the genome, described by the chromosome it sits on and its position along that chromosome. A locus can be as small as a single base or as large as a whole gene or region. The plural is loci. Giving a locus precisely requires stating which reference assembly the coordinates refer to.

Technical explanation

A locus is defined by genomic coordinates, and coordinates are meaningful only with respect to a named assembly, because positions shift between assembly versions. Analysts therefore record the assembly alongside any coordinate and convert between assemblies explicitly rather than assuming equivalence. Loci may be described functionally as gene loci, regulatory loci or association loci. In genome-wide association studies a reported locus is usually a region of linkage disequilibrium rather than a causal base, so the lead marker marks a neighbourhood in which the causal variant may lie, and fine-mapping is required before any gene is implicated. In pathogen genomics, loci are the typing targets of schemes such as multilocus sequence typing, where defined gene fragments are compared across isolates. A practical limitation is that paralogous and repetitive sequence can make locus assignment ambiguous: reads from one region may map to another, producing apparent variants that are artefacts of position rather than of sequence.

Practical example

An analyst receives a variant list in coordinates from an older human assembly and lifts it over to the current one before comparing with a clinical database. Two variants fail to convert because the surrounding region was reorganised between assemblies, so the analyst inspects those positions manually instead of silently dropping them from the comparison.

Common misunderstanding

A locus is often confused with a gene. Many loci lie outside genes entirely, in regulatory or intergenic sequence, and an association signal at a locus does not identify which gene it acts on. A further error is quoting coordinates without the assembly version, which makes a position ambiguous and can send a reader to the wrong part of the genome.
