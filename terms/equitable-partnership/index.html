Equitable research partnership

Ethics and data governance Published

Definition

An equitable research partnership is a collaboration in which the partners share decisions, resources, recognition and data rather than one group supplying samples while another does the science. It covers who sets the question, where the sequencing happens, who analyses the data, who is named on the paper, and who controls what happens to the data afterwards.

Technical explanation

Equitable partnership has become a central concern in global genomics because the historical model, in which samples and data flow from one setting to analysis and publication in another, produces unequal capacity and distorted research agendas. Features commonly specified in partnership frameworks include joint development of the research question, budgets that allocate real resources to all partners, generation and analysis of data in the collecting country where feasible, authorship criteria agreed in writing before work begins, co-ownership or co-stewardship of data, investment in training and in durable infrastructure, and a published partnership agreement. Regional initiatives have shown that building analytical capacity alongside data generation changes who can use the resulting resources. Equity also has a scientific dimension: datasets dominated by participants of European genetic ancestry limit the accuracy of variant interpretation and of polygenic scores in other populations, so broadening participation under equitable terms improves the science as well as its distribution. Obstacles include funding rules that favour institutions in high-income countries, short project cycles and informal authorship practices that are hard to challenge after the fact.

Practical example

Two institutions planning a population genomics study agree before any samples are collected that library preparation and sequencing will take place at the collecting institution, that both sites will run the primary analysis independently and reconcile results, and that first and senior authorship positions will be allocated according to a written plan covering each planned paper.

Common misunderstanding

Partnership is sometimes measured by the presence of a local co-investigator on a grant. Nominal inclusion without control of budget, data or authorship is not equity. It is also mistaken to treat equity as a purely ethical concession: narrow participation weakens variant interpretation and risk prediction for most of the world's population, so unequal partnerships produce weaker science.
