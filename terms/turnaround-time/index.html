Turnaround time

Sequencing and laboratory methods Published

Definition

Turnaround time is how long it takes from a sample arriving, or being collected, to a result being issued. In genomics it covers extraction, library preparation, sequencing, analysis, interpretation and reporting. Different parts of that chain dominate for different tests, and a result that arrives too late to inform a decision has limited practical value.

Technical explanation

Turnaround time is a service performance measure defined by the start and end points chosen, so stating them matters: collection to report, receipt to report, and sequencing to data release describe very different intervals. In sequencing workflows the contributions are usually batching delay while enough samples accumulate, library preparation, instrument run time, computational analysis, interpretation by scientific staff, and authorisation. Which step dominates depends on the setting. In high-throughput surveillance, batching and transport often outweigh the run itself; in rare disease diagnostics, interpretation and multidisciplinary review are usually the longest components; in rapid critical care applications, the entire chain is reorganised around speed. Reducing turnaround time involves trade-offs rather than pure gain: smaller batches raise cost per sample, shallower sequencing reduces sensitivity, and compressed interpretation increases the risk of error, so targets should be set against the decision the result informs. Reporting a median alone hides the tail, and it is the delayed minority of cases that usually determines whether a service is clinically or operationally useful.

Practical example

A genomic surveillance programme finds that although its sequencing run takes two days, the interval from sample collection to a shared consensus genome is far longer, because specimens are transported weekly and sequenced in large batches. Moving to more frequent, smaller batches shortens the overall interval, at a higher cost per sample, and makes results useful during an investigation.

Common misunderstanding

Turnaround time is often equated with instrument run time, as though faster sequencing alone would deliver faster results. In most services the laboratory run is a minor component compared with transport, batching, analysis and interpretation. Quoting only the median also conceals the delayed cases, which are usually the ones where timeliness mattered most.
