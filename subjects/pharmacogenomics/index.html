Pharmacogenomics

How inherited variation affects the way medicines are processed and tolerated, the nomenclature used to describe it, and the guidelines that translate genotype into prescribing advice.

12 entries. Open these in the searchable dictionary.

  • Adverse drug reaction (ADR)

    An adverse drug reaction, abbreviated ADR, is harm caused by a medicine given at a dose used for treatment or prevention. It differs from a medication error, where the wrong drug or dose was given. Some reactions are predictable extensions of the drug's action; others are rare, severe and immune-mediated, and a few are strongly associated with inherited variation.

    Pharmacogenomics Published

  • CPIC guideline

    A CPIC guideline is a published document from the Clinical Pharmacogenetics Implementation Consortium that explains what to do with a pharmacogenomic test result once it exists. It does not advise whether to order a test; it translates a genotype into a predicted phenotype and sets out prescribing implications, with the strength of evidence stated for each recommendation.

    Pharmacogenomics Published

  • CYP2C19

    CYP2C19 is a liver enzyme involved in processing several widely used medicines, including some that reduce stomach acid, some antidepressants and a drug that prevents blood clots by being converted into its active form. Inherited versions of the gene range from no activity to increased activity, which alters how much active drug a person ends up with.

    Pharmacogenomics Published

  • CYP2D6

    CYP2D6 is a liver enzyme that processes a substantial share of commonly used medicines, including some painkillers, antidepressants and heart medicines. The gene encoding it is unusually variable: people can carry versions with no activity, reduced activity, or extra copies that increase activity. This variability makes it one of the most studied pharmacogenes.

    Pharmacogenomics Published

  • DPYD

    DPYD encodes the enzyme that breaks down fluoropyrimidine chemotherapy drugs such as fluorouracil and capecitabine. People who carry variants reducing this enzyme's activity clear the drug more slowly and face a much greater risk of severe toxicity. Testing for a small set of these variants before treatment is recommended in several health systems.

    Pharmacogenomics Published

  • HLA-B*57:01

    HLA-B*57:01 is one version of an immune system gene that presents fragments of proteins to T cells. People carrying this version are at high risk of a serious hypersensitivity reaction to the antiretroviral medicine abacavir. Testing for it before the medicine is started is one of the earliest and clearest examples of pharmacogenomics in routine practice.

    Pharmacogenomics Published

  • Metaboliser status

    Metaboliser status is a summary of how quickly a person is predicted to process a particular medicine, based on the pair of gene versions they carry. The usual categories run from poor, through intermediate and normal, to rapid and ultrarapid. It is a prediction from genotype, not a measurement of the drug in the body.

    Pharmacogenomics Published

  • Pharmacogenomics (PGx)

    Pharmacogenomics studies how differences in a person's DNA affect the way their body handles medicines and how those medicines act. Inherited differences can change how quickly a drug is broken down, how strongly it works, or how likely a severe reaction is. The abbreviation PGx is used for both pharmacogenomics and the closely related term pharmacogenetics.

    Pharmacogenomics Published

  • Pre-emptive pharmacogenomic testing

    Pre-emptive pharmacogenomic testing means testing a person's relevant genes before any particular medicine is being considered, and storing the result for future use. Because inherited genotypes do not change, one test can inform prescribing decisions for years. The alternative, reactive testing, happens only when a specific medicine is about to be prescribed.

    Pharmacogenomics Published

  • Star allele nomenclature

    Star allele nomenclature is the naming system used for versions of genes that affect how medicines are processed. Each named version, written with an asterisk and a number such as star two, stands for a defined combination of variants inherited together on one chromosome. It names a haplotype rather than a single DNA change.

    Pharmacogenomics Published

  • TPMT and NUDT15

    TPMT and NUDT15 are two genes whose protein products help the body handle thiopurine medicines, used in some leukaemias, inflammatory bowel disease and immune conditions. People with reduced activity of either protein are at much higher risk of severe suppression of blood cell production. Testing before treatment is established practice in many health systems.

    Pharmacogenomics Published

  • Warfarin pharmacogenomics

    Warfarin thins the blood and has a narrow margin between too little effect and too much. How much a person needs varies widely, and part of that variation is inherited. Variants in a gene that processes the drug and in a gene encoding its target together explain a meaningful share of the differences in dose requirement.

    Pharmacogenomics Published
